## Revision Task
1.  **Assess New Sources**: The reviewer will sometimes investigate the literature, and find that there are CRITICAL MISSING PAPERS in your source list. When this happens, these papers will be added as new verified sources in your input for the revision. Pay attention to whether there are CRITICAL MISSING PAPERS in the Reviewer's report, and if there are, strongly consider incorporating the conclusions from those new verified sources into your report.
2.  **Validate Criticisms & Look for Concordance**: Critically examine the feedback.
    - **Concordance**: If both reviewers agree on a flaw or insight, prioritize it.
    - **Disagreement**: Use skepticism where reviewers differ. Check the primary sources (context) to decide which reviewer is correct.
    - **Substantiation**: If a criticism is valid and substantiated by the data, update your draft accordingly.
3.  **Reject Invalid Feedback**: If re-examining the primary source confirms that your original analysis was correct—and a reviewer is mistaken—you are instructed to **ignore** that specific piece of feedback. Do not compromise accuracy to appease the reviewer.
4.  **Weight the Selectivity Reviewer on the grade**: For the **selectivity grade specifically**, give the Selectivity Reviewer's recommendation and cited panel evidence strong weight — it examined the off-target panels in depth. You are **not obligated** to adopt its grade: if the verified sources contradict it, you may override with a stated reason. It has **no authority over the verdict or the target**; disregard any of its commentary on those.
5.  **Draft Revision**: Generate the final revised report, integrating all valid improvements. Update verdict, target, and selectivity from your draft if warranted based on your revisions here. 
6.  **No Revision Commentary**: The final report must read as a polished, standalone document. Do NOT include any references to "previous drafts," "corrections made," "removed citations," or any other revision history. Simply present the final, accurate information as if it were always the intended content—just like a published book contains no trace of its editing process.
